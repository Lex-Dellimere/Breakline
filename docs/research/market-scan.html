Breakline · desk research · Australia · 31 Oct 2026

Is Breakline new, and will it make money in Australia?

The packaging is new; the idea has close relatives in OT and healthcare vendor access. The risk is real and current. The money is plausible but small, and it runs through MSPs. Nothing found yet shows that anyone will pay.

1Verdict

  1. New in packaging, not in concept. I found no product that sells client-held, scheduled or approved gating of any MSP RMM tool as an SMB network box. Close analogues exist (Imprivata Customer PAM "Gatekeeper", OT vendor-access gates, Microsoft Customer Lockbox), so "nobody has built this" is not true. "Nobody sells it to Australian clinics" may be true, and may also mean there is no demand.
  2. The problem is real and current. ASD's ACSC issued an alert on 19 Aug 2026 for N-central exploitation in Australia, and the chain kept going into September. That is evidence of risk. I found no evidence of willingness to pay.
  3. Money is plausible but small and channel-bound. At AUD 149/site/month you need about 68 sites for AUD 10k MRR and about 672 for AUD 100k MRR (about 0.5% of the 68,325 Australian businesses with 20-199 staff, if they average 2 sites; the 2-sites figure is my assumption). Clinics are tiny (dental averages 3.1 staff) and the MSP controls the network edge, so distribution is the hard part, not the box.
  4. Two product risks could kill it: blocking the RMM channel outside windows breaks the 24/7 patching and monitoring that MSPs sell, so MSPs may veto it (my inference, untested); and DNS plus firewall gating is bypassable (the N-central attackers dropped Cloudflare tunnels).
  5. Recommendation: treat it as unproven. Run about 10 interviews (section 10) and a traffic measurement with 1-2 MSPs before building more. I would not expect AUD 100k MRR from direct-to-clinic sales. AUD 10k MRR through 3-5 MSP partners is the realistic first target. That last line is my judgement, not a sourced fact.

What it means for the build

My reading of the findings, not part of the research.

  • Lead with closed-by-default, not with auto-close. In the N-central case the ACSC alert came about 19 days after N-able noticed the problem, so a feed-triggered close would have been late. What would have protected a client is that the channel was already closed outside booked windows.
  • The biggest open product question is MSP automation. If a tool's patching and monitoring use the same names as its remote control, closing the channel stops patching too. The monitor-only pilots should measure exactly this: how much RMM traffic happens outside windows, and to which hosts. A per-tool split between management and remote control may be needed.
  • Breakline narrows the attacker's window; it does not remove a foothold. The tunnels in the N-central campaign kept access after the RMM was cut off. Sales material should say this plainly.
  • Phase 6 is the right next build. The scan says to run interviews and a traffic measurement before building more. Phase 6 produces the monitor-only pilot box that measurement needs; features beyond it should wait for the interviews.

2Is it new?

What Breakline combines (from docs/BRIEF.md and docs/THREAT_MODEL.md): a client-owned off switch for any RMM or remote-control tool; DNS NXDOMAIN plus firewall drop at the edge (no endpoint agent); booked windows plus passkey approval; auto-close on CISA KEV or ACSC advisories; a hash-chained audit log exported as PDF; the MSP needs to install nothing.

What already exists, by closeness:

Closest analogue What it does Gap versus Breakline
Imprivata Customer PAM "Gatekeeper" The customer installs a Gatekeeper and controls when the vendor can connect: enable or disable now, for N hours, until a date, or on a weekly access schedule. Vendors can request access and the customer approves, denies or time-limits it by email. Source: X Imprivata docs. Software, not an appliance. The vendor must connect through Imprivata, so it cannot gate an arbitrary RMM the MSP already runs. Enterprise-priced healthcare product. This is the best proof the concept is valid.

Verdict on novelty (my judgement): - Novel: the specific combination of generic RMM catalogue, edge enforcement with no endpoint agent, client-owned switch, and KEV-triggered auto-close, aimed at Australian SMBs. I did not find that combination. - Not novel: client-controlled, time-boxed, approved vendor access. It is a known pattern in OT, healthcare vendor management and Microsoft's own cloud. - Where "new" is weakest: the incumbents that would be easiest to extend are the RMM vendors, Huntress or Blackpoint (client-facing "approve my MSP" console), and Imprivata.

Search limits. My web search tool is US-centric. I did not search Australian distributor catalogues (Dicker Data, Ingram Micro), Crunchbase or Product Hunt, or open the Fortinet and Meraki feature documentation. A small Australian vendor could exist that I missed. Do those checks before telling anyone "nobody does this".

3.2 The Aug to Sep 2026 N-central campaign: verified

  • ACSC alert, first published 19 Aug 2026 F. ASD's ACSC "has observed the targeting of vulnerabilities affecting the N-able N-central product within Australia". CVE-2026-18556 and CVE-2026-18577 are authentication bypasses affecting all current versions including 2026.3. Patches came out on 1 Aug 2026 and Hotfix 2 on 6 Aug. The alert says it has no information that a specific industry is targeted, and tells small and medium business to ask their MSP whether it uses N-central.
  • Attacker behaviour F iTnews, 20 Aug 2026: the attackers connected to managed devices with Take Control and registered Cloudflare tunnel services to keep access after N-central access was revoked. A search summary of Help Net Security says Huntress saw one self-hosted instance affecting one partner and nine downstream client organisations, and that targets included domain controllers and backup servers S; second-hand.
  • It kept going into September X Huntress, updated 6 Sep 2026: N-able issued Hotfix 4 for CVE-2026-86218, an actively exploited pre-auth RCE rated CVSS 10.0. Huntress also published a proof of concept for a further chain (CVE-2026-86206 and CVE-2026-86207). The Huntress post says "a compromised N-central server can be used to run scripts, push tools, and open remote sessions across every downstream endpoint it manages".
  • KEV listing of the 2026 CVEs: unverified. One search summary says CVE-2026-18577 is in KEV. I did not check the catalogue or the date. Feed latency matters for Breakline's auto-close: the ACSC alert came about 19 days after N-able noticed licensing anomalies on 31 Jul 2026 S, so an ACSC-triggered close would have been late. The earlier Aug 2025 N-central KEV entry was added on 13 Aug 2025 S, reported as the day of the patch.
  • The threat model's "Aug-Sep 2026 N-central campaign" is supported. Its statement that attackers "used the RMM's own remote-control feature to reach client machines" is supported by iTnews and Huntress.

5.2 Who decides the purchase

  • RACGP Standards 6th edition, criterion F8 F: a practice needs a person, either a team member or a contracted provider, with primary responsibility for the security of its electronic systems. If that person is external, a team member must hold digital governance. Contracts should set out the provider's obligations "including for remote access", and practices should use standard letters of agreement signed by ICT providers.
  • Reading: the decision usually sits with the practice owner or manager on paper, but the technical authority is often the MSP. A vendor blog says the MSP supplies the technical answers on insurance forms while the owner signs S, secondary.
  • Hypothesis to test in interviews, not a finding: the practice manager or owner holds the budget, and the MSP holds a veto in practice because it controls the network edge, the RMM and the support relationship.

7Channel

Via MSPs. - Precedents that MSP-led security distribution works in Australia: Guardz launched in ANZ in Feb 2024 to let MSPs "secure and insure" SMBs S; Cyber Threat Insure launched an MSP distribution model in Apr 2025, citing only about 20% of Australian SMBs holding standalone cyber policies (its own claim) S; Airlock's AustCyber grant targets MSP/MSSP reach S; ThreatLocker has MSP bundles S; Twingate and Tailscale run MSP programmes X. - Why MSPs may resist (my inference, no MSP evidence found): the product's trust model is that the MSP is an adversary or a risk. It could break unattended automation (patching, scripts, monitoring), add approval latency to after-hours fixes, and expose the MSP's access to audit. My searches returned no MSP statements either way about client-held gating. This is the key unknown. - Why MSPs might sell it: after N-central, "ask your MSP if they use N-central" is ACSC's own advice to SMBs. Vendors frame approval and zero standing access as good practice (Microsoft frames MSP zero standing access this way; several guides recommend customer sign-off, silence not counting as approval) S. An MSP could use the box to prove it is not the weak link. I found no survey or case study showing MSPs win business this way. - Deployment dependency: a two-port inline box that redirects client DNS and drops traffic sits at the network edge, which the MSP usually manages. A client-bought box probably still needs the MSP's cooperation to install. This is my inference and should be tested.

Direct to client. The practice manager or owner would have to buy and install a network box against their MSP's advice. No evidence found that clients are asking for this.

Brokers and associations. Cyber insurance brokers and underwriting agencies are the strongest theoretical channel if an insurer rewards the control (not currently found). RACGP, state GP bodies and Primary Health Networks publish cyber guidance for practices (the search surfaced a South Western Sydney PHN page on RACGP resources S). Whether any would endorse or distribute a commercial box is unverified. Practice-management associations, ADA, AVA, CPA Australia and the law societies are untested hypotheses.

The buyer has to believe the client-owned switch, the audit PDF and the auto-close are worth AUD 1,800 per year. - Unverified willingness to pay. I found no evidence of what an SMB would pay for this.

9Risks (why this could fail to make money)

  1. No demonstrated demand. No survey, pilot or competitor revenue evidence. The absence of direct competitors may mean no buyers.
  2. MSP veto. The MSP controls the edge, the RMM and the client relationship. A box that locks it out outside windows has to be framed as a benefit, and no evidence supports that yet.
  3. Breaks the MSP service. RMM agents do patching, scripts and monitoring around the clock. Gating the RMM channel outside windows stops that. If remote-control relays cannot be separated from agent check-in for a tool (e.g. ScreenConnect's relay is shared), the gate is all-or-nothing. Inference, to be measured with real traffic.
  4. Bypassable. The N-central attackers installed Cloudflare tunnels for persistence (iTnews). A catalogue-based DNS and firewall gate only covers tools and domains in the catalogue. The threat model already lists this as residual risk (T1, T2, T15).
  5. Auto-close latency. KEV and ACSC feeds lag. In the 2026 incident the ACSC alert came about 19 days after the vendor noticed exploitation (see 3.2). KEV timing for the 2026 CVEs is unverified.
  6. Tiny buyers. Clinics average a few staff, with no IT budget and no dedicated security owner. The mid-sized multi-site subset is not sized.
  7. Weak regulatory pull. Nothing mandates or rewards this control; insurers ask about MFA, not about client-held gating.
  8. Good-enough substitutes. RMM consent prompts, UniFi/Fortinet rules, ThreatLocker, Huntress RMM Guard (free to Huntress customers). Any one lets the MSP say "we have that".
  9. Incumbent response. An RMM vendor, Huntress or Imprivata can ship a client-facing approval layer faster than a startup can sell hardware.
  10. Hardware logistics. Shipping, site installs, returns, and support across multiple sites. This costs money in a market where the buyer will compare against a AUD 190 gateway.
  11. Trust and liability. If the box fails closed on an emergency (a vendor needs urgent access), or fails open, the founder carries blame. Pilot terms need to be explicit.
  12. Unverified key facts. AU presence of several vendors, Kaseya/Datto consent features, ACSC MSP-guide specifics, Privacy Act details from law-firm blogs.

10Interview scripts

Mom Test rules for both: ask about the last time something happened, not about opinions of Breakline. Do not describe the product until the end, and then only if they ask. Ask for specifics (names, dates, dollars). Seek commitments (time, intros, money), not compliments. Take notes on what they did.

10.1 Clinic practice manager or owner (about 30 minutes)

  1. Tell me about the last time someone outside your practice (IT provider, software vendor, anyone) needed to get into your computers. Who, how, and when was it?
  2. How does IT support work day to day here? Who do you call, who signed the contract, and when does it renew?
  3. Last time your IT provider connected remotely, did you know beforehand? How did you find out?
  4. Do you know which remote tools your IT provider and your software vendors use to connect? How would you find out?
  5. Who in the practice decides what an outside provider can and cannot access? Walk me through the last time that came up.
  6. Have you ever had an IT security scare, outage or breach, or heard of one nearby? What happened, what did it cost, and what did you change after?
  7. Did anyone (IT provider, insurer, software vendor, peers) contact you about the N-central or other remote-tool news this year? What did you do?
  8. The last time you renewed cyber insurance, who filled in the form? What did it ask about remote access or IT providers? Did anything change in the premium or conditions?
  9. Last accreditation or privacy review: what was asked about IT security? Who prepared the evidence, and how long did it take?
  10. Have you ever limited, paused or asked to approve your IT provider's access? Tell me what happened. If not, what stopped you?
  11. What have you paid for in the last 12 months to improve security (tools, audits, training, hardware)? How was it chosen, who approved it, and what did it cost?
  12. When something needs to be plugged into your network (firewall, wifi, backup), how is it decided and installed? Who does the work?
  13. Last time you needed IT fixed after hours or urgently, what happened and how long did it take?
  14. Who else has remote access to your systems (practice software, imaging, pathology, phones)? Can I see how that is arranged? Who else should I talk to?

10.2 MSP owner or service manager (about 30-40 minutes)

  1. Tell me about your client base: how many clients and sites, which sectors, and what RMM and remote tools you run. How did you choose them?
  2. Walk me through the last time you remoted into a client site. How was it started, how did the client know, and where is it logged?
  3. Roughly what share of your RMM activity is automated (patching, scripts, monitoring) versus a technician starting a session? How do you know?
  4. What did you do when the N-central advisory landed in August, or the last time any RMM advisory hit you? Who found out, how long did patching take, and did clients contact you?
  5. Has a client ever asked you to restrict your access, approve sessions, or show session logs? What happened?
  6. Last time a client's insurer, auditor or accreditor asked about your access controls, how did you answer, and how long did it take?
  7. Have you lost or won a client over a security question? Tell me the specific case.
  8. What sits at your clients' network edge (firewall brand), who owns the admin credentials, and who is allowed to change it? Last time you installed a new device at a client, what was the process, time and cost?
  9. What security tools did you add in the last 12 months? How did you decide, what did it cost you, and what did you charge clients?
  10. How do you price security add-ons (bundled or line item)? What happened the last time you raised prices?
  11. How often do you need urgent access outside planned maintenance windows? Describe the last time.
  12. Have you worked with any client-side restriction on your access (client VPN approval, bastion host, change windows, GDAP/PIM)? What was the friction, and how did you handle it?
  13. How do you evaluate and onboard a new product (trial, pilot, requirements, margin)? What was the last one you said yes to, and what tipped it?
  14. Who else do you rely on for advice or purchasing (distributor, vendor reps, peer group)? Who would you introduce me to?

10.3 Recruiting the first 10 interviews in Australia (plan, not yet done)

  • Mix: 5 MSPs and 5 clinics or professional offices. Start with 3 MSPs (they are gatekeepers and will surface the objections), then the clinics, then the remaining MSPs. Aim for at least 2 states and a mix of clinic types (GP, dental, vet, allied health) plus 1-2 accountants or law firms to test breadth.
  • Qualifying filters: clinics with roughly 10+ staff, 2+ sites or a dedicated IT provider; MSPs with 20+ clients that include clinics or professional services. Prefer at least two MSPs that use N-central or ScreenConnect.
  • Sources: 1. The founder's own network and the MSP the founder already uses, then ask each interviewee for two introductions. 2. LinkedIn searches for "practice manager" at clinic groups and "MSP owner" or "service delivery manager" in Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide and Perth. 3. Primary Health Networks and state GP or dental bodies that already circulate cyber guidance. Ask for an introduction, not an endorsement. Whether they will help is unverified. 4. MSP communities and vendor user groups (N-able, ConnectWise, Kaseya, Ninja user groups; r/msp), and Australian cyber and channel events. Specific groups and dates are unverified. 5. Practice-management and accounting-industry networks (names to be checked before outreach).
  • Message: "Researching how clinics and their IT providers handle remote access after this year's N-central news. 20-30 minutes, nothing to buy, no pitch. Happy to share what I learn." Offer a gift card or a summary of findings. Do not mention Breakline in the invite.
  • Logging and decisions: record what they did last time, any money spent, any commitments (intro, pilot offer, repeat meeting), and quotes about objections. Review after 5 interviews and again after 10.
  • Ethics: do not collect patient data, do not share other interviewees' names, and tell people what you will do with notes.

11Next steps for validation

  1. Do the 10 interviews (section 10) before more build. Suggested decision signals (my proposals): - Continue if at least 3 of 5 MSPs say they would accept a client-held gate in some form, and at least 3 of 5 clients have changed or spent something after an RMM or MSP scare. - Rethink the product if 3+ MSPs say the RMM traffic outside business hours is mostly automation they will not pause. - Stop or pivot if nobody has paid for anything comparable and nobody will commit time to a pilot.
  2. Measure real traffic. Run monitor-only on 1-2 MSP offices and the MSP's own sites. The key number is what share of RMM traffic happens outside business hours and what it does.
  3. Close the desk-research gaps: - Check the CISA KEV date for CVE-2026-18577 and the other 2026 N-central CVEs, to size feed latency. - Read the ACSC "engaging an MSP" guide in full. - Check Imprivata CPAM availability, pricing and any Australian reseller. - Check Fortinet and UniFi documentation for domain-based scheduled policies. - Check Kaseya VSA and Datto RMM consent features. - Find a primary source for the RACGP practice count and for the AU presence of DNSFilter, Cisco, BeyondTrust, CyberArk, Delinea and Zscaler. - Look at Dicker Data and Ingram Micro catalogues for anything similar.
  4. Test the insurance angle. Ask 2-3 brokers or underwriting agencies whether any insurer would reduce a premium or change a question for a client-held gate on MSP access. This could change the channel picture.
  5. Test the price. In interviews, ask what they paid for their last security add-on rather than asking what they would pay for this.
  6. Decide the kill date. If interviews and the traffic test do not show a buyer within about 8 weeks, stop building for sale and keep the repo as a research artefact.
