# Breakline buyers and channels: MSPs to interview, rooms to be in, amplifiers, and gap checks

Desk research only, 1 November 2026 (searches run 31 Oct to 1 Nov). I read public web pages only. I contacted no one, signed up for nothing, filled in no forms and downloaded nothing. Organisations only: no personal phones, emails or home details, and no individuals' names.

Companion to `market-scan.md` (sections 1, 7, 9 and 10 were read first; nothing from it is redone here).

**Emphasis (updated 1 Nov 2026):** the business has decided the MSP is the customer ("a lock outside your RMM", deployed at the MSP's clients' sites, priced per client site). So the effort here goes into sections 1 (MSPs), 2 (where MSPs gather), 4 (insurers, brokers and distributors that work with MSPs) and 5 (KEV timing). Section 3 (clinic groups) is deliberately short: they are now second-order, useful mainly as the MSP's end clients.

RMM-signal confidence used in section 1:

- **HIGH**: the MSP's own text, or a vendor page, names the tool.
- **HOST**: the MSP's "remote support" link points to a product-branded hostname (for example `x.screenconnect.com`) but no text names the tool. Probably right, not proven.
- **LOW**: unnamed platform, logos only, or inferred.
- **NONE**: not found.

**Headline findings**

1. Across 35 MSP sites plus reserves, **no MSP publicly names N-able N-central or Take Control**. The N-able partner locator returned 404 and no job ad naming N-central turned up. This is odd because an older vendor-sourced article says Australia has more N-central MSPs per capita than any other country [X] https://itwire.com/it-industry-news/strategy/n-able-growing-its-msp-base . The tools that do show: ScreenConnect (most common: about 9 of the 35, mostly by hostname), NinjaOne (Trucell, Epic IT by name; Evolve by hostname), Kaseya stack (Teamwork Technology), ConnectWise RMM (Matrix Solutions), Atera (Melbits), TeamViewer and AnyDesk (one each). To find N-central users you will have to ask, not search.
2. **No MSP mentions the ACSC MSP Partner Program (MSP3)**, and I could not confirm MSP3 still exists as a distinct program (section 2.3).
3. **KEV was fast.** CISA listed the N-central bugs 2 to 3 days after exploitation began or the patch shipped, and about 15 to 16 days before the ACSC alert (section 5.1).
4. **Datto RMM already has a switch an end user can hold** (Privacy Mode). That is the closest incumbent feature to a client-held gate, and the first thing a Kaseya-family MSP will say (section 5.2).
5. **No Australian product launched in 2025 to 2026 that does client-controlled, scheduled or approved MSP access** was found (section 5.4). Treat as not found, not as proof of absence.
6. **ACSC's own MSP guidance already tells customers to schedule MSP remote access for agreed times and tie it to a job ticket** [X] (section 4.1). That is a policy hook an MSP can quote to its own clients.
7. **Two MSPs in the table already market access transparency.** PIP (Sydney) tells clients "you approve every session" and that session logs are available on request [F]; Epic IT (Perth) used the N-central news in a 6 Sep 2026 article to tell clients what to ask their MSP about console access, MFA and breach notification, then offered a security review [F]. That is evidence an MSP will sell this story, and a warning that some MSPs will say they already do it.
8. **N-central framing is already in the market:** the ACSC's 19 Aug alert told SMBs to ask their MSP whether it uses N-central [F via Cyber Daily]. MSPs who do not use it are using that as a differentiator (Epic IT), so those who do use it have a reason to show proof.

What this means for Breakline's feed-triggered auto-close:

- **First KEV listing came 2 days after exploitation began** (1 Aug to 3 Aug) and **16 days before the ACSC alert** (3 Aug to 19 Aug; 15 days for CVE-2026-18556). An ACSC-only trigger would have fired 18 days after first observed exploitation. A KEV trigger would have fired in 2 days. So the earlier scan's worry (ACSC late) is real, but KEV is the faster feed and should be the primary trigger, with ACSC as a backstop.
- For CVE-2026-86218, KEV came **3 days after Hotfix 4** and 2 days after N-able said it was exploited.
- **Limits:** the first 2 days (including a weekend) are not covered by any feed; KEV lists on US business days, so the AU-side time is later; and the KEV entry names "N-central", while the attackers used **Take Control** to reach managed devices (per the earlier scan's iTnews evidence). The catalogue mapping must therefore close the N-central console, the agent path and the Take Control relay domains together, not just one name.
- **CVE-2026-86206 and CVE-2026-86207** (a further chain, patched in 2026.3 HF3 / 2026.4 per a Dataminr summary [X]): the Cyber Centre notes open-source reporting of exploitation of 86207 [F]. Search results show **no CISA KEV listing for either**: Wiz's pages mostly say not in KEV, one Wiz page cites a Reddit post for a 11 Sep deadline (weak), and Action1 says all three are being exploited. I could not open CISA's own catalogue entries for them, so treat "not in KEV" as **unverified**. Practical point: a KEV-only trigger would not fire on an exploited bug that has no KEV entry yet; ACSC and vendor advisories are a backstop.
- **Precedent from 2025:** CISA added the earlier N-central bugs CVE-2025-8875 (insecure deserialization) and CVE-2025-8876 (command injection) on **13 Aug 2025** [F] https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2025/08/13/cisa-adds-two-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog . A KEV trigger would have closed N-central on day zero of public disclosure. That is two N-central incidents in 12 months where KEV landed within 0 to 3 days of the patch or exploitation signal.
- **Design implication for a per-site product sold through MSPs:** the auto-close fires on a product match, so every MSP using N-central loses remote access at every protected site on the day of a listing, until they patch and an approver reopens. That is the thing an MSP will ask about (my inference; worth testing in interviews as the "emergency access" question).
