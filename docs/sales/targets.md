# First targets

The first five MSPs to contact, from `docs/research/buyers-and-channels.md` (the full list has 35
and its sources). Each has a first email that opens with something true and public about them.
The rules in `outreach.md` apply: you send it, from your own address, only after reading it.

**Order:**
1. Your dad's IT provider. It's warm, so it comes first.
2. Then PIP, Health IT and Trucell, which will raise the hardest objections early.
3. Then CRT.
4. Then Teamwork Technology.

At most 5 emails a week.

**To ask every MSP on the call** (after the open questions):
- Do you use Datto Privacy Mode, Kaseya "Require Permission", ScreenConnect consent or Take
  Control "Request User Permission" today?
- What would happen to your service if remote access closed the day your RMM appeared in
  CISA's exploited list?
- Who at the client would approve a session?
- How much of your remote traffic could we tell apart: technicians' remote control versus
  agent check-in, patching and monitoring? Would you run a monitor-only trial to measure it?
- Who do you know that runs N-central? None of these five is known to use it, and N-central is
  the case that started this.

## 1. PIP (Sydney, medical IT): pip.com.au, contact via pip.com.au/contact-pip

Why them: their remote-support page tells clients "you approve every session" and that session
logs are available on request. That is our promise. They either back it with something like
Breakline, or there's a gap between the promise and practice.

**Subject:** Your "you approve every session" promise

> Hi PIP team,
>
> I read on your remote support page that clients approve every session and can ask for the
> logs. That's rare, and it's exactly what I'm researching. I'm building a small security tool
> for Australian MSPs, and since the N-central news in August I've been asking MSPs how they
> keep remote access under the client's control.
>
> Could I have 20 minutes with whoever runs your remote support? I'd like to hear how that
> approval works day to day. No pitch, and I'll share what I hear from other MSPs (no names).
>
> [your name], [phone]
